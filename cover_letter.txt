Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit our manuscript entitled "Self-consistent methodology
for SEM magnification linearity assessment using 2D Fourier analysis of a
holographic grating: detector comparison, spatial characterisation, and open
benchmark dataset" for consideration in Measurement Science and Technology.

This work presents a self-consistent methodology for assessing SEM
magnification linearity and detector equivalence that does not require a
certified reference standard. The key insight is that the 2D fast Fourier
transform (FFT) of a periodic artefact provides an internal frequency
reference---termed Internal Frequency Referencing (IFR)---whose invariance
across magnifications and detectors serves as a relative consistency check.
The methodology is demonstrated on a commercial holographic diffraction
grating (Thorlabs GH13-06U, 600 lines/mm) imaged on a JEOL JSM-IT700HR
field-emission SEM at four magnifications (400x-1000x) using both
secondary-electron (SED) and backscattered-electron (BED-S) detectors
(1500 images total). The key contributions are:

1. The 2D FFT yields an invariant pitch of 1.707 um across all magnifications
   and detectors (SNR > 150), demonstrating relative magnification consistency
   to within 0.06%. This Internal Frequency Referencing (IFR) principle is
   transferable to any imaging instrument where a periodic reference structure
   is available.

2. The 1D profile-minima method gives systematically smaller values
   (1.643-1.648 um), with the -0.063 um offset attributed to the
   non-sinusoidal groove intensity profile. An independent optical diffraction
   measurement (lambda = 635 nm), performed at the geometric limit of the
   reflection setup, gives 1.65 +/- 0.21 um (k=3), consistent with both
   methods.

3. SED and BED-S detectors yield statistically equivalent pitch values
   (bootstrapped Bland-Altman bias <= 0.013 um, < 0.8%) across all
   magnifications (56-353 paired tiles).

4. Gaussian process regression confirms significant but weak, large-scale
   spatial components at 400x-750x (likelihood-ratio p <= 0.018; smooth-field
   amplitude <= 0.94% of the pitch) and a flat field at 1000x.

5. A GUM-compliant uncertainty budget with properly separated Type-A
   (within-image repeatability) and Type-B components (pixel quantisation,
   FOV calibration, scan distortion), supplemented by non-parametric
   bootstrap confidence intervals (10 000 replicates, 95% BCa).

The complete dataset (1500 images, per-image pitch values, tile-level
spatial metadata) is publicly archived on Zenodo
(https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20641961), and the full R analysis pipeline
is available on GitHub
(https://github.com/dawidkucharski/sem-grating-pitch-benchmark),
together constituting an open benchmark for SEM distortion assessment
methods. Both repositories are released under CC-BY 4.0.

I confirm that this work is original, has not been published previously, and
is not under consideration elsewhere. There are no conflicts of interest to
declare.

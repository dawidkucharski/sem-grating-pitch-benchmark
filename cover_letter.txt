Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit our manuscript entitled "Self-consistent SEM
magnification linearity assessment using a holographic grating: Fourier
analysis, detector comparison, and open dataset" for
consideration in Measurement Science and Technology.

This work presents a self-consistent methodology for assessing SEM
magnification linearity and detector comparison that does not require a
certified reference standard. The fast Fourier transform (FFT) of the
column-averaged intensity profile of a periodic artefact provides an
internal frequency reference---termed Internal Frequency Referencing
(IFR)---whose invariance across magnifications and detectors serves as a
relative consistency check. The methodology is demonstrated on a
commercial holographic grating (Thorlabs GH13-06U, 600 lines/mm) imaged
on a JEOL JSM-IT700HR field-emission SEM at 400x-1000x with
secondary-electron (SED) and backscattered-electron (BED-S) detectors
(1500 images). The key contributions are:

1. The sub-bin-refined FFT yields a pitch of 1.644 um across all
   magnifications and detectors (SNR > 150), demonstrating relative
   magnification consistency to within 0.4%, conditional on the
   instrument-reported pixel scale. Bootstrap confidence
   intervals (10 000 replicates) show that the observed 750x scale step
   (+0.244% in relative pixel-scale terms) is statistically robust
   beyond sampling uncertainty.

2. The 1D profile-minima method (1.643-1.648 um) agrees with the FFT
   value to within 0.005 um; the -0.063 um artefact of unrefined
   integer-bin FFT quantisation is identified and resolved.

3. No practically relevant detector-dependent pitch bias was detected:
   median paired SED-BED-S differences are 0.0000 um at all
   magnifications (bootstrap 95% CIs within +/-0.0013 um), and
   per-detector FFT medians agree to within 0.0003 um. The small
   mean-based Bland-Altman biases (<= 0.013 um) are shown to be carried
   by a disclosed 4% tail of algorithmic outliers in the SED profile
   analysis.

4. Gaussian-process regression confirms significant but weak large-scale
   spatial components at 400x-750x (likelihood-ratio p = 0.018 to
   1e-54) and a flat field at 1000x; stage-coordinate regression bounds
   the field linearity to 0.5%.

5. A GUM-compliant uncertainty budget with separated Type-A and Type-B
   components and expanded uncertainties (k = 3) of 0.09-0.21 um,
   supplemented by 10 000-replicate bootstrap BCa confidence intervals
   and normalised-error compatibility metrics (En <= 0.23) against
   the optical-diffraction measurement (1.65 +/- 0.21 um, k = 3) and
   the white-light-interferometric cross-check (1.6651 um; 0.1 nm
   scatter; the certified machine lateral-scale contribution to the
   pitch is about 1.6 nm at k = 3).

The processed dataset and the complete R and Python pipelines are
publicly archived on Zenodo (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20641960)
and GitHub (https://github.com/dawidkucharski/sem-grating-pitch-benchmark),
both under CC-BY 4.0, constituting an open dataset and a reproducible
benchmark resource for SEM pitch and distortion analysis. Supplementary
material comprises the synthetic-image
validation figure and the corresponding per-pitch numerical results.
